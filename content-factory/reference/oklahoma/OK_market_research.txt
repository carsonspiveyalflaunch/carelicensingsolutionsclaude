Section 1. Executive Summary
1.1 Opportunity Snapshot
Oklahoma DD group home services support residents with intellectual and developmental disabilities
(IDD) in community-based homes of 4–12 people, funded primarily through the state's Section 1915(c)
Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers. The program is in a structural expansion
phase: the DDS waitlist has been reduced from approximately 13 years (2022) to roughly one year
(2026), and the state has processed over 3,000 waitlist families into services through eleven cohorts as
of early 2026, with Cohort 12 (729 individuals) entering service this spring.
This expansion has been accompanied by direct rate increases. Effective October 1, 2024 (formalized
in Administrative Letter 24-02) and finalized for Community Living group homes on February 24, 2025
(Administrative Letter 25-02), per diem rates for T1020 group home services were increased across all
bed sizes and home types.

Section 2. Service Model & Regulatory Framework
2.1 Program Names and Classifications
Oklahoma does not use a "Level 1 / Level 2 / Level 3" residential habilitation classification. (The prior
report fabricated this structure.) Group home services under the Oklahoma DDS HCBS Waivers are
organized by two parallel factors:
   •​   Home type — Traditional, Community Living, or Alternative Living
   •​   Bed count — 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, or 12 residents
Both factors are coded under a single procedure code (T1020) and are differentiated through the
service authorization rather than through HCPCS modifiers. The provider's authorization ("PB number")
issued by DDS Provider Support locks in the applicable rate.

Three Group Home Types Defined by OAC 317:40-5-152
Per OAC 317:40-5-152(e), three types of group home services are provided through HCBS waivers:

 Type                      Description
 Traditional group         Serve no more than 12 members. Lower-intensity supports. Per OAC
 homes                     340:100-6.
 Community living          Serve no more than 12 members whose needs cannot be met in a less
 homes                     structured setting; require regular, frequent, and sometimes constant
                           assistance with daily living. Higher rate to reflect higher staffing
                           intensity.
 Alternative living        Up to 4 residents. Eligibility requires meeting criteria per OAC
 homes                     340:100-5-22.6 and approval by the DDS Director or designee. Highest
                           per-resident rate to reflect smaller-scale staffing economics.


2.2 Waiver Authority and Funding Streams
Oklahoma DDS administers four 1915(c) HCBS waivers under which DD group home services may be
authorized:
   •​   Community Waiver — for individuals age 3 and older with intellectual disability or related
        condition; the primary pathway for DD group home placement.
   •​   Homeward Bound Waiver (OK.0399) — for class members certified in Homeward Bound et al. v.
        Hissom Memorial Center (U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Oklahoma, Case No.
        85-C-437-E).
   •​   In-Home Supports Waiver for Adults (IHSW-A) — supports for adults living in their own/family
        home; group home services are generally not provided under IHSW.
   •​   In-Home Supports Waiver for Children (IHSW-C) — supports for children ages 3–17 living at
        home.
⚑ VERIFY: Specific CMS waiver numbers for the Community Waiver and IHSWs are subject to periodic
renumbering with each renewal/amendment. The prior report cited "OK.0179.R07.00" for the Community Waiver,
which could not be verified against current CMS records. Confirm current waiver numbers with OHCA Long-Term
Care Services at oklahoma.gov/ohca before relying on them in any submission.

Funding Flow
Federal funds flow from CMS under 1915(c) authority → OHCA (state Medicaid agency) → operational
management by OKDHS DDS. Providers bill OHCA directly under fee-for-service for DDS waiver
services. The October 1, 2024 rate structure was set through the OHCA State Plan Amendment Rate
Committee (SPARC) and is uniform statewide.

2.3 SoonerSelect Carve-Out for DDS Waiver Services
This is one of the most important regulatory facts for a new provider, and one the prior report got wrong:
Oklahoma launched the SoonerSelect managed care program in April 2024, contracting with three
MCOs (Aetna Better Health of Oklahoma, Humana Healthy Horizons of Oklahoma, and Oklahoma
Complete Health) for the general SoonerCare population. However, DDS HCBS waiver services —
including T1020 group home services, T2033 Daily Living Supports, S5151 respite, and related
residential codes — are carved out of SoonerSelect and continue to be reimbursed fee-for-service
through OHCA's Medicaid Management Information System (MMIS).
Practical consequence: a new DD group home provider does NOT need separate contracts with the
three SoonerSelect MCOs to bill for these services. The prior report's instruction to negotiate with each
MCO was incorrect and would have wasted significant time.
⚑ VERIFY: Confirm in writing with OHCA Provider Services (405-522-6205) before going live, in case the
carve-out scope changes.

2.4 Regulatory Oversight Structure
Primary: OKDHS Developmental Disabilities Services
DDS is the certifying authority for DD group homes. Important: DDS, not the Oklahoma State
Department of Health (OSDH), is the relevant agency. OSDH's "Residential Care Home" licensure
under OAC Title 310 Chapters 675/680 applies to a different category of facility (adult care homes /
assisted living) and is NOT the correct pathway for DD group homes. The prior report's licensing
checklist erroneously routed providers through OSDH.

Secondary: Oklahoma Health Care Authority
OHCA is the state Medicaid agency; it administers SoonerCare, enrolls Medicaid providers, processes
claims, and sets reimbursement rates through SPARC. All providers must hold an OHCA SoonerCare
contract in addition to DDS certification.

Federal: Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services
CMS oversees the underlying 1915(c) waivers and enforces the HCBS Settings Rule (42 CFR
441.301(c)). Federal Medicaid integrity rules (42 CFR 455 et seq.) apply to all providers, including
ownership disclosure and exclusion screening requirements.

2.5 HCBS Settings Rule Compliance
The federal HCBS Settings Rule (42 CFR 441.301(c)(4)) sets baseline requirements that apply to every
DD group home receiving HCBS waiver funding. Key requirements include:
   •​   Setting is integrated in and supports access to the greater community.
   •​   Setting is selected by the individual from among setting options.

   •​   Setting ensures individual rights of privacy, dignity, and respect, and freedom from coercion and
        restraint.
   •​   Setting optimizes individual initiative, autonomy, and independence in making life choices.
   •​   Setting facilitates individual choice regarding services and supports, and who provides them.
   •​   Provider-owned/controlled residential settings have additional requirements: individuals have a
        lease or other legally enforceable agreement, privacy in their sleeping unit including lockable
        doors, choice of roommate, freedom to furnish and decorate, control of schedule and visitors,
        access to food at any time, and physical accessibility.
New providers should document HCBS Settings Rule compliance in their initial site approval
submission to the DDS Residential Unit. Any restriction on individual rights must be supported in the
member's Individual Plan (IP), with documented justification, alternatives tried, and time limits.

3.4 DDS Residential Unit Site Approval
Each physical group home must be separately approved by the DDS Residential Unit before service
delivery begins. This is in addition to provider certification.

Submission
   •​   Contact: DDS.Residential@okdhs.org
   •​   Pre-licensure site visit by DDS staff.
   •​   Local fire marshal certificate of compliance.
   •​   Local zoning confirmation in writing.
   •​   Documentation of HCBS Settings Rule compliance for the physical setting.
   •​   ADA accessibility documentation.

Standards Reviewed
The site review draws on OAC 340:100-6 (Group Home Staffing and Oversight), OAC 340:100-5-22
(Community Residential Supports), and the HCBS Settings Rule. Areas typically inspected include
bedroom and bathroom configurations and privacy, common areas, kitchen and food storage, secured
medication storage, accessibility, fire safety systems, emergency egress, and member rights postings.

3.5 Common Application Delays
Without access to a published DDS deficiency study, the most defensible items to flag are those that
arise across nearly all licensure regimes. Plan around:
   •​   Incomplete or stale background checks (especially when checks expire mid-review).
   •​   Policies and procedures that are templates rather than tailored to the specific operation.
   •​   Ownership disclosure gaps (particularly indirect ownership > 5% in parent/holding companies).
   •​   Insurance certificates that don't name the right entities or have inadequate limits.
   •​   Local zoning or fire marshal sign-off missing or out of date when the site visit occurs.
   •​   Staffing plan that doesn't match the home size or member acuity in the proposed authorization.

Section 4. Reimbursement & Funding
4.1 How Rates Are Set
DDS group home rates are set centrally through OHCA's State Plan Amendment Rate Committee
(SPARC) and are uniform statewide. There are no geographic adjustments. The rate structure has been
informed by the 2023 Guidehouse rate study, with adjustments implemented via Administrative Letters.
All DD group home services bill under procedure code T1020 per resident per day. The applicable rate
is determined by:
   •​   Home type (Traditional, Community Living, or Alternative Living)
   •​   Number of beds (4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, or 12)
The rate is locked in by the service authorization ("PB number") that DDS Provider Support issues for
each member-home pairing. The provider bills the per-resident-per-day rate via OHCA's MMIS;
occupancy adjustment is already built into the rate, not separately reimbursed.

4.4 Authorization Process
Once you are an active DDS provider and a member's team selects your home through
person-centered planning, the DDS Provider Support Center issues a "PB number" — the service
authorization that defines who, where, what service code/modifier set, and for how long. The PB
number is what the OHCA MMIS will pay against when you submit claims.
If a home's occupancy or population changes, the authorization is end-dated and a new authorization is
created at the appropriate rate tier. Administrative Letter 24-02 specifically called out this process:
"Group Home Authorizations require end dating the current authorization and creation of a new
authorization effective Oct. 1 with the new rate. This project will be completed by the DDS Provider
Support Center."

4.5 Billing
Claims are submitted electronically through OHCA's MMIS or through the OHCA provider portal at
ohcaprovider.com. Standard Medicaid timely filing rules apply. Electronic funds transfer (EFT) is
mandatory for payment.

Section 5. Referral Pipeline & Demand
5.1 Waitlist Status (Verified)
Oklahoma's DDS waitlist reduction is the most significant demand-side story in this market. The
relevant verified facts as of early 2026:
   •​   In May 2022, the Oklahoma Legislature appropriated historic funding to end a then 13-year wait
        for DDS waiver services.
   •​   As of April 2026, the wait time has been reduced to approximately one year from application.
   •​   More than 3,000 waitlist families have been approved to receive services or are already
        receiving services.
   •​   Cohort 12 — 729 individuals who applied between January and June 2025 — began processing
        in April 2026.
   •​   DDS held 32 in-person or virtual Regional Family Meetings through early 2026, with additional
        meetings planned through 2026.

5.2 Cohort System
DDS divides applicants into cohorts by application date. After cohort assignment, applicants typically
take 6–9 months to transition onto services once contact is made and the application is approved;
eligibility documentation completeness can compress this to a few weeks or extend it. Each new cohort
represents a wave of demand entering the system.
For prospective group home providers, the cohort system is useful intelligence: it tells you
approximately how many people are entering services each quarter and where to focus capacity
decisions.

5.3 The Three Entry Points to Your Home
Group Home Vacancies Page
DDS maintains a self-service Group Home Vacancies portal at
oklahoma.gov/okdhs/services/dds/ghv.html where licensed providers log in to update bed openings.
This is a primary discovery channel for DDS Case Managers and families and should be kept current
as a matter of routine.

DDS Case Manager Relationships
Members and families select providers through person-centered planning facilitated by their DDS Case
Manager. Case Managers are organized by regional area office (Metro Oklahoma City, Tulsa, Eastern,
Western). Building relationships with the Case Manager population in your service area is the single
highest-leverage business development activity.

Direct Family Outreach
Families attending Regional Family Meetings, often during their cohort's preparation phase, are actively
learning about providers. DDS publicizes meeting schedules through its website and through Liberty of
Oklahoma.

Section 6. Program Operations
6.1 Staffing Structure
DDS group home staffing is governed by OAC 340:100-6 (group home staffing and oversight) and OAC
340:100-3-38 (staff training requirements). The core roles are:

Program Coordination Staff (PCS)
Defined under OAC 340:100-5-22.6 and OAC 340:100-6. The PCS oversees the group home's daily
operations, supervises direct support staff, coordinates with case managers, and conducts monitoring
visits to the home. Specific qualification, supervision, and frequency requirements are set out in OAC.

Direct Support Professionals (DSP) / Habilitation Training Specialists (HTS)
The day-to-day direct care workforce. Minimum qualifications per OAC 340:100-3-38: 18 years of age,
high school diploma or equivalent, complete required pre-service training before unsupervised service
delivery, clear background screening.

Registered Nurse (RN)
Required for clinical oversight, medication administration delegation, and health monitoring. The RN
can be employed or contracted. The RN cost is already built into the T1020 rate's underlying
methodology.
⚑ VERIFY: Specific staff-to-resident ratios and required RN coverage hours vary by home size and member
acuity and are not consistently published in summary form. Confirm with the DDS Residential Unit during site
approval.

6.3 Therap and Documentation
Therap is the mandated information management system for OK DDS providers. Required
documentation flows include:
   •​   T-Logs: daily service delivery notes by direct support staff.
   •​   ISP Data tracking: progress against Individual Plan goals.
   •​   MAR: medication administration record.
   •​   GER (General Event Reports): incident reports across four severity tiers.
   •​   Service authorization tracking: linking PB numbers to delivered services.
A new provider should plan for Therap onboarding (provisioning, super-user training, staff training) in
parallel with policies and procedures development rather than at the last minute.

The four-tier incident reporting structure is documented in the OK Community Waiver / Homeward
Bound Waiver applications submitted to CMS. Tier-specific reporting timelines, escalation rules, and
review responsibilities apply. New providers should obtain the current GER tier definitions and matrix
during Therap onboarding and incorporate them into the abuse/neglect/exploitation and incident
response policies.

Section 7. Market Conditions
7.1 Demand Side
The demand picture is straightforward and well-documented through DDS public communications:
    •​   Waitlist reduction has accelerated the rate of new members entering services since 2022.
    •​   Approximately 3,000+ families have been approved into services across 11 cohorts; Cohort 12
         (729 individuals) is processing in April–May 2026.
    •​   Demographic factors (aging adults with IDD whose family caregivers are aging out,
         transition-age youth from school systems) sustain steady residential demand independent of the
         waitlist.
    •​   Subsidized expansion of services to underserved populations (rural areas, tribal communities,
         medically complex members) is a stated policy direction.

7.2 Supply Side
Supply-side intelligence requires more caution. Public sources do not consistently publish provider
counts, market shares, or competitive analyses, and the prior report's specific numbers (62 providers,
named market leaders with percentages) could not be verified. What can be stated with confidence:
    •​   Provider names and contracts are listed publicly at
         oklahoma.gov/okdhs/services/dds/providerlist.html. Count from this list for current network size.
    •​   Vacancies by home are listed publicly at oklahoma.gov/okdhs/services/dds/ghv.html. Surveying
         current vacancy patterns gives a real-time supply signal.
    •​   Direct Support Professional workforce remains a structural constraint statewide, exacerbated by
         competing wages in retail, hospitality, and warehousing.
    •​   DSP+ — Oklahoma's direct support workforce incentive program — provides incremental
         compensation funded through Medicaid; this is a real source of workforce capacity that didn't
         exist a few years ago.

7.3 Workforce
DSP recruitment and retention is the single most important operating challenge cited by DD providers
nationally and in Oklahoma. The state has responded with rate increases, the DSP+ incentive program,
and the Called to Care recruitment campaign launched in March 2024. Through FY24 reporting, 14,665
individuals received $16.6 million in DSP+ incentive payments.
⚑ VERIFY: Specific DSP wages vary significantly by region, employer, and tenure; the BLS Occupational
Employment Statistics for Oklahoma is the most defensible public source for wage benchmarking. Do not rely on
the specific salary numbers from the prior report ($12.42/hour HTS, $21.19/hour case manager) without verifying
against current data.

7.4 Regulatory Direction
Recent regulatory and policy direction has favored expanded community-based supports over
institutional care, consistent with federal HCBS Settings Rule expectations. Specific Oklahoma
developments include:

Section 8. Startup Financial Analysis
8.1 Approach to This Section
The prior report's financial analysis is the section most damaged by the underlying rate fabrications. A
break-even of "63 clients" makes no sense in a market where group homes physically cannot exceed
12 residents and where actual revenue per resident is 3–8× what the prior report assumed.
This corrected analysis uses the verified rate schedule from Section 4 and provides revenue projections
at three home types. It does NOT attempt to publish authoritative cost or break-even figures, because
operating costs vary enormously by location, member acuity mix, and staffing strategy, and any specific
cost number would just be a different kind of fabrication.

8.3 Cost Framework (Categories, Not Numbers)
Rather than fabricate specific cost numbers, this report describes the cost structure DDS itself models
in its rate methodology, so providers can build their own pro forma against the real cost categories:
    •​   Direct support wages and overtime — typically 50–60% of operating costs in residential
         settings. Awake staff during active hours, sleep-shift staff overnight, substitute hours for PTO
         and training (DDS rate methodology assumes ~25 days PTO and ~30 hours/year training per
         staff).
    •​   Supervision — Program Coordination Staff supervising the home; DDS methodology assumes
         specific supervisor-to-DSP ratios.
    •​   Clinical support — RN and service coordination hours; the DDS rate methodology assumes
         hours equivalent to supervisor hours.
    •​   Administrative add-on — 19% of direct service cost in the DDS rate model.
    •​   Program support — 12.9% of direct service cost in the DDS rate model, covering supplies,
         transportation, and indirect support.

    •​   Facility — rent or mortgage, utilities, maintenance, insurance, taxes.
    •​   Member-related — food, household supplies, transportation, recreation.
The 19% administrative + 12.9% program support figures come directly from the Guidehouse 2023 rate

8.4 Realistic Break-Even Considerations
Break-even for a DD group home is not a number of "clients" — it's an occupancy threshold against
fixed costs. Practical anchors:
    •​   Fixed costs (PCS, supervisory RN time, facility, administrative overhead) are largely incurred
         whether you have 4 or 8 residents.
    •​   Variable costs (additional DSP coverage, food, supplies) scale with census.
    •​   A 6-bed Community Living home generating ~$443K/year at 85% occupancy generally supports
         a viable operation; a 6-bed Traditional home generating ~$193K/year requires extremely tight
         cost discipline given similar fixed staffing needs.
    •​   Alternative Living Homes (4 residents at $427/day = ~$530K/year) economically support a
         smaller-scale operation with higher per-resident intensity.
⚑ VERIFY: Build your own pro forma against the rate type you plan to pursue. Do not use the prior report's
$250,000 first-year loss projection — it was built on a $31.60/day rate that doesn't exist.

8.5 Capital Requirements Categories
Categories of startup investment that any DD group home will incur, listed without specific dollar
estimates because the spread is too wide to be useful:
    •​   Entity formation and legal — typically modest ($500–$2,000).
    •​   Property acquisition or lease deposit and any renovation/accessibility build-out — by far the
         largest variable; can range from $20,000 (modest lease deposit + minor work) to $500,000+
         (purchase plus renovation).
    •​   Insurance binder costs — general liability, professional liability, workers' comp, auto, property.
    •​   Background checks and credentialing fees — small per-person costs that compound across
         initial staff cohort.
    •​   Initial staff recruitment, hiring, and pre-service training.
    •​   Therap onboarding (provisioning, training).
    •​   Working capital — at minimum 3 months of operating expenses; lenders typically want to see
         4–6 months.
    •​   Furniture, fixtures, household setup, vehicles.

Section 9. Risks & Considerations
9.1 Operational Risks
  •​   Workforce instability. DSP turnover is the single most cited operational challenge in DD
       residential services nationally. Building a retention strategy (competitive wages, career ladders,
       recognition, scheduling) into the operating model from day one — not retrofitting it after turnover
       hits — is structurally important.
  •​   Member acuity drift. A member's needs can escalate over time; your home's rate tier and
       staffing model may not flex automatically with that. The transition from Traditional to Community
       Living requires a new authorization.
  •​   Single-home concentration. A new provider operating a single home has limited resilience to a
       single bad outcome (loss of a member, a serious incident, key staff turnover). Diversification
       across multiple homes or service lines reduces this, but increases compliance complexity.

9.2 Compliance Risks
  •​   Background check lapses. Staff cleared 5 years ago but never re-screened is a common audit
       finding; the OSBI check renewal cycle is 5 years.
  •​   Federal exclusion exposure. Failure to run monthly OIG-LEIE and SAM.gov screening on staff,
       contractors, and the entity itself is a documented Medicaid compliance gap that can trigger
       payment recovery.
  •​   Incident reporting timeliness. The four-tier Therap GER system has tier-specific reporting
       timelines; missed timelines are routinely flagged in DDS monitoring.
  •​   HCBS Settings Rule violations. Restrictions on members' rights (locked refrigerators, restricted
       visiting hours, restricted phone access) without IP-documented justification are systematic
       violations of the Settings Rule and a current focus of federal oversight.

9.3 Financial Risks
  •​   Rate static, costs growing. DDS rates change via Administrative Letter — not annually, not on a
       predictable cycle. Cost increases (wages, insurance) can outpace rates and erode margin
       without recourse other than waiting for the next state-level rate review.
  •​   Authorization timing. Revenue follows authorization; if an authorization is delayed or end-dated
       due to a member transition, the bed sits empty even though fixed costs continue.
  •​   Single-payer concentration. Nearly all DD group home revenue flows through OHCA. There is
       no payer diversification.
  •​   Claim denials and recoupment. Documentation gaps surface as billed-vs-paid variances or audit
       recoupments later.

9.4 Regulatory Direction Risks
  •​   Federal HCBS rule changes. The Settings Rule remains a moving target as CMS issues
       guidance and enforcement varies by region.
  •​   State-level managed care expansion. Although DDS waiver services are currently carved out of
       SoonerSelect, scope decisions are revisited periodically; a future inclusion of waiver services in
       managed care would require provider operational adaptation.

Section 10. Strategic Recommendations
10.1 Before You Commit Capital
   •​   Email STO.DDSD.PROVIDERSUPPORT@okdhs.org with a single request: "Please send the
        current Provider Application packet, the most recent residential rate schedule, and any
        administrative letters that have superseded AL 24-02 / 25-02." Whatever they send is the
        canonical version. Treat any conflicts between this report and their packet as resolved in favor
        of their packet.
   •​   Read OAC 340:100-6 (Group Homes) and OAC 317:40-5-152 (Group Home Services
        Definition) directly. They are short, governing, and free.
   •​   Talk to one or two existing DDS group home operators. The trade association (Oklahoma
        Association of Community Providers, oacp.org) and the OK Developmental Disabilities Council
        (okddc.ok.gov) are reasonable entry points.

10.2 Choosing Home Type and Bed Count
The single most important strategic decision is matching home type and size to the operating model
you can realistically execute. Some heuristics:
   •​   Traditional 12-bed maximizes total revenue but compresses per-bed economics; success
        depends on tight cost control and staffing scale efficiencies.
   •​   Community Living 6–8 bed offers the most favorable revenue-per-bed economics with
        manageable scale, and aligns with current state policy preference for community integration.
   •​   Alternative Living 4-bed offers the highest per-bed rate ($427/day) but requires DDS Director
        approval and is intended for members whose needs cannot be met in larger settings — not for
        every applicant.
   •​   Specialty positioning (medically complex members, members with intensive behavioral needs
        requiring Extensive Residential Supports) opens the highest-rate tiers (T2033 TG = $1,046/day
        for ERS) but requires correspondingly intensive clinical capacity and credentialing.

10.5 Quality-First Positioning
In a market where the state is actively expanding capacity through cohort processing, new providers
with strong quality records have a structural advantage. Specific signals that build credibility with Case
Managers and families:
   •​    Low staff turnover and tenured direct support staff.
   •​    Clean Therap GER record — few incidents, fast resolution, documented learning.
   •​    Demonstrable HCBS Settings Rule compliance (lockable bedrooms, member control of
         schedule and visitors, individualized food access, lease/residency agreements).
   •​    Positive family and member satisfaction signals.
   •​    Specialization in an underserved niche (medically complex, behavioral health, transition-age
         youth, cultural/linguistic competency for specific communities).
